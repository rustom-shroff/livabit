# OAP 0.1.0 authorization and local consent

Status: OAP 0.1.0 agreed at G1 on 2026-10-04. Date: 2026-10-03. Normative terms and common records are defined in [OAP](oap.md). This contract implements OAP-FR-002/003/006/010 and OAP-PR-001/002/004/006/008. It does not define a hosted identity, subscription or consent service.

## A1. Independent authorities

Four decisions remain independent: operating-system permission to observe permitted usage metadata; the user's device-local monitoring and recipient-sharing consent; the chosen receiver's authentication/connection permission; and the agent's own standing instructions and communication preferences. A skill, received activity record, receiver response or previously issued credential MUST NOT grant monitoring permission, widen sharing scope or authorize a device action.

The device is the sole provider-side consent authority. A receiving endpoint MAY retain its own authorization policy and previously received data, but MUST NOT become an authority for granting device collection/export. No online consent replica, lease or synchronization request is necessary to complete a local pause or revocation. The initial contract exposes no device-modifying capability.

## A2. Local grant and effective scope

Before OAP collection or export, the device MUST have a persisted affirmative monitoring grant and an affirmative recipient-sharing grant. A sharing grant binds the authenticated local user, the device, one chosen receiver, its profile and verified destination, a recipient-scoped stream identity, an app allowlist, permitted event families and fields, reporting cadence, retention, a monotonically increasing local scope revision, a start time and a finite expiry. The wire envelope's identifiers report this binding; submitted identifiers do not establish authority. The local user/device binding IDs and transport profile are not added to activity envelopes.

Only `activity.summary` is available in 0.1.0. Each active grant allows at most 128 app identities. An empty app allowlist means no permitted apps and MUST stop queries for that recipient. The default sharing lifetime is 24 hours; the local subscription lifetime MUST NOT exceed the grant's expiry. Monitoring permission and sharing permission MUST be shown separately. The device MUST expose the effective approved scope before activation.

For a recipient, effective scope is the intersection of local user approval, OS availability, selected profile capability and the locally configured subscription. A receiver capability cannot add an app or field. Requested limits MAY be stricter than the contract defaults; they MUST NOT relax protocol maxima. Device-wide collector scope is the union of independently active approved recipient scopes, with each export filtered again for its particular recipient. Denied app names, counts, totals, labels and derived categories MUST NOT enter that recipient's record or diagnostics.

Raw observations and derived aggregates MUST obey original-age retention independently of whether an event is successfully delivered. Retry, credential rotation, subscription renewal and copying into a new record MUST NOT refresh their age. Goals, assistant memory, content, URLs, messages, passwords and coaching responses are outside provider scope.

## A3. Begin, pause, resume and change scope

Collection/export begins at the grant's approved start, never from earlier OS history. For a window crossing a consent boundary, the device MUST aggregate only known, separable authorized intervals. If only an inseparable whole-window aggregate exists, omit its values and report missing coverage. Do not prorate totals to estimate permitted activity.

Pause MUST atomically persist a state denying new affected collection, local retained-history reads, event publication, dispatch and retry. Previously authorized retained payloads MAY remain until their existing expiry, inaccessible while paused. Resume begins a new permitted observation segment; paused intervals MUST NOT be backfilled. An unchanged reporting window MAY be finalized with explicit partial coverage, but MUST NOT imply continuous observation.

Narrowing MUST atomically advance the scope revision, stop affected operations and purge all retained/queued payloads from the old stream, including those whose app fields would fit the narrower scope. Previously finalized windows MUST NOT be reissued under fresh IDs as corrections or projections in 0.1.0. New activity uses the new scope epoch, stream identity and window identity, with usable credentials and receiver admission setup for that epoch before operations resume. Already received records may remain at the receiver. Scope widening requires fresh on-device user consent and a new revision; renewal alone MUST NOT widen scope.

A lifetime-only renewal is idempotent for the same locally supplied operation identifier and requested scope/lifetime. It MAY extend a subscription only within the still-approved monitoring and sharing policy. It MUST preserve finalized record bodies, original retention deadlines and the stream sequence for an unchanged scope. If the sharing grant expired, collection/export stays stopped until a new affirmative grant becomes active; no interval without active permission is backfilled.

## A4. Revocation and the commit boundary

Revocation/disconnection MUST atomically mark the local grant revoked, advance its revision, purge affected pending/retained recipient payloads and remove usable receiver credentials. The device MUST persist the change before showing local success. It MUST survive process restart, reboot and lack of network access. One recipient's revocation MUST NOT revoke unrelated recipients. Turning global monitoring off stops all OAP collection and affected operations.

Every read or send attempt MUST acquire permission against the current committed consent state immediately before it begins. Queued work MUST recheck current scope/revision; an earlier scheduling decision is insufficient. A transaction or equivalent fence MUST prevent a new operation from beginning after a denying consent commit. Operations dispatched before the commit may still complete or arrive. A network cancellation is best effort and MUST NOT be described as recalling received data.

Optional receiver-native credential revocation/unsubscribe MAY follow local disconnection if the host supports it. It MUST NOT be required to finish the local operation, and no remote acknowledgement delays the local fence. Keep only a bounded, content-free revocation marker through the old credential's expiry, then remove it. The receiver's copies, OS usage history and other independently authorized recipients are subject to their own policy; provider deletion makes no claim about those copies.

## A5. Receiver authentication and pairing

The selected profile MUST specify how the user obtains actual receiver-issued credentials and verifies the destination. [Direct HTTPS](profiles/direct-https.md) uses an opaque per-device, per-recipient expiring bearer credential from the chosen receiver's existing secure setup. It is a reference interface for compatible receivers, not a claim that a consumer agent already implements it. The profile's setup MUST bind the token to the recipient, device and destination, and provision the permitted stream/subscription/scope epoch in the receiver's admission registry. The receiving implementation MUST validate this server-side binding and expiry before accepting records; it MUST NOT trust the body's IDs as authentication.

The device may allocate prospective recipient, stream and subscription IDs before activation without collecting activity. Through the receiver's existing authenticated setup, the user supplies the non-secret binding IDs and approved scope, then securely obtains the issued token and expiry. This is a setup exchange, not an invented OAP HTTP endpoint. The receiver's admission registry is a restriction on its own acceptance, not a remote device consent authority. A new scope epoch requires a supported update/re-pairing there before dispatch; absence of that setup makes the destination unavailable. Pause/revocation on the device still completes offline without updating the receiver.

Pairing consists of selecting a supported receiver inside the chosen agent infrastructure, securely obtaining its endpoint and scoped credential, verifying its TLS identity and destination policy, authenticating capability discovery, and explicitly approving the local effective scope. A URL alone is insufficient. A receiving endpoint that forwards activity through a separate service to another agent is excluded. Receiver-native OAuth MAY be defined by a later host-specific profile using that host's documented discovery, audience, scopes, refresh and revocation. Do not invent OAuth endpoints or operate an OAP/liveabit authorization server.

For an opaque reference credential, the device stores its actual issued expiry and endpoint binding separately from event payloads. Its issued lifetime MUST be at most 30 days; 24 hours is recommended. This bounds minimal revoked-credential marker retention without extending local consent. Credential validity and local consent validity are both required; one does not extend the other. Rotate/reissue through the receiver's supported setup. Automated refresh is unavailable unless explicitly defined and verified by the selected profile. Failure to obtain compatible credentials means the destination is unsupported or needs user attention, not that a forwarding bridge should be added. A later provider-native authentication profile may have different reviewed lifetimes; it is not implicitly enabled by this reference contract.

## A6. Secrets, integrity and failure states

Credentials MUST be kept in an OS or vetted protected secret store, addressed from local configuration through an opaque handle. Source control, event bodies, URLs, command-line arguments, fixtures and diagnostics MUST NOT contain live credentials. Synthetic secrets used by the isolated simulator MUST be visibly labelled as test-only. Receiver token verification SHOULD use a vetted authentication implementation, hashed token storage and constant-time comparisons; token issuance MUST use a cryptographically secure random generator.

Authenticated TLS provides the direct profile's transport confidentiality, integrity and receiver identity. Event IDs plus the receiver's durable deduplication ledger prevent a successfully consumed retry from being interpreted twice within the required ledger horizon. TLS replay-sensitive early data is disabled for event publication. A later webhook profile must use its actual host-supported signing and verification rules rather than attaching an invented OAP signature. Payload labels remain untrusted data and cannot change authorization.

Unknown or corrupt consent, denied OS access, grant/subscription expiry, unusable credentials, identity verification failure and incompatible capabilities MUST fail closed for the affected recipient's data operations. Network loss alone leaves authorized local collection and bounded retention possible, but MUST NOT delay pause or revocation. Show these states separately from a healthy connection; an authentication failure is not an empty activity window or proof of no usage.

## A7. Required evidence

Conformance must exercise absent/expired consent, multiple disjoint recipients, each mid-window boundary, pause/reboot/resume without backfill, offline revocation during concurrent dispatch, narrowing/purge, non-widening renewal, invalid body identities, wrong/expired credentials, secret redaction and corrupted local state. M2 additionally proves real receiver onboarding, credential rotation or reissuance, direct topology and the intended existing agent's response. Simulator credentials and a local receipt do not establish production host support. See [acceptance criteria](../acceptance-criteria.md), especially AC-02 through AC-05 and AC-15 through AC-19.
