# OAP delivery and recovery

Contract: 0.1.0. Date: 2026-10-03. Status: agreed at G1 on 2026-10-04. All state on the provider side resides on the device. Normative requirements refine [OAP](oap.md); runtime evidence is separate.

## D1 Provider lifecycle

Local pairing authenticates a chosen final receiver, checks its exact capabilities, then asks for device approval of the effective scope. Pairing alone collects/exports nothing. An active grant and subscription, available collection permission and usable bound credentials are required. State is `active`, `paused`, `revoked`, `expired` or fail-closed `unknown`. Collection can be `available`, `denied` or `unavailable` independently; healthy transport does not restore denied collection.

On creation, persist grant, subscription, scope revision, randomly generated stream identity and idempotency records atomically. At most four recipients, four active sharing grants and one active subscription per recipient are permitted on a device in this initial profile. A separate global monitoring permission is not a fifth recipient grant. Do not accept activity before approved_at or after the grant deadline. Once a window closes and coverage is known, redact approved data, form all pages, check bounds, assign IDs/sequences and freeze canonical bytes in one transaction. Finalize a window only once. Durable state contains the window-finalization marker, original deadlines, page bytes, sequence counter and fresh-attempt budget. A crash cannot turn a duplicate finalization into another event. Collection checkpoints and suppressed windows also remain bounded by retention.

Before a new read, selection, collection checkpoint, first dispatch or retry begins, hold a local consent-generation fence and validate current scope/state/expiry/credential binding. The local pause/revoke/narrow transaction increments that generation, prevents new affected operations from starting, clears affected retained data/credentials as appropriate, persists the change and cancels cancellable work. Operations already in flight when the transaction commits can finish remotely; their bytes cannot be recalled. Reading retained data under an old grant is not an exception. Reboot/network loss never resumes a revoked subscription. See [authorization A4](authorization.md#a4-revocation-and-the-commit-boundary).

Pause blocks sharing, retained-data reads and retries; collection for that paused recipient also stops. Existing retained data may remain only within its unchanged age/byte limits, but pause-to-resume needs an explicit local action and fresh active checks. Do not collect/backfill the paused interval. Resume keeps already attempted immutable events eligible only if still permitted; its first new observation window begins at or after resume and reports consent-boundary coverage if needed. Narrowing rotates the scope epoch, purges old affected whole windows and invalidates old cursors. Widening never occurs through renewal. Disconnect is local revocation plus credential and activity deletion; remote unsubscribe/key invalidation is optional best-effort cleanup, never the local authority.

Renewal leaves finalized pages' original expires_at unchanged. It can extend future local subscription operation within an active explicitly renewed grant and bound credential, but never past a local grant deadline or enlarge consent. A repeated still-live operation key returns its original result without restoring inactive authority; operation identities themselves cannot provide new authority. Keep at most 64 creation/renewal idempotency entries per recipient, through their associated grant's original expiry and no more than 24 hours. Do not evict still-live keys to admit more operations; reject new creation/renewal if full. After that horizon a stale grant-bound request is invalid_request and cannot create a new grant. Pause/narrow/revoke are inherently repeatable state transitions and cannot be blocked by an idempotency quota. Bound control/index state is included in the per-recipient storage quota.

## D2 Identities, consumption and ordering

First validated receipt atomically inserts `(authenticated recipient, stream_id, event_id, canonical SHA-256 digest)` into a durable consumer ledger and updates its observation model. Exact repeats return duplicate without applying duration again; different bodies sharing an ID are rejected. Independent receivers must make that transaction safe under concurrent duplicates and restart. JSON identity fields never override authentication context. Receiver state keys are scoped to the authenticated recipient/device binding, avoiding collisions across accounts.

Sequence expresses publisher assignment within a stream, not global order, wall-clock certainty or attention. On receiving sequence 7 before 6, a consumer records the missing range and can consume permitted observation data conservatively. It MUST NOT drop 6 merely because 7 arrived first or let late observations overwrite current state. Same sequence with different event identity in one stream is `conflicting_duplicate`. Streams from different devices/recipients/scope epochs have no comparable sequence order. A stream reset is explicit through a new bound identity, never counter reuse.

Whole-group state validates shared metadata, the fixed partition map and all pages as described in [P5](oap.md#p5-coverage-and-pagination). Ledger retention MUST last at least 24 hours after first valid consumption and through event.expires_at + 300000 ms, whichever is later. A receiver advertises/preserves this horizon. Once an event is expired, replays are rejected even if the ledger later evicts its ID. Ledger/group storage is bounded per principal; capacity exhaustion rejects admission with retryable storage_unavailable before acknowledging consumption. Receiver rate/capacity limits are independent of provider collection limits.

## D3 Publication budget, batching and retries

The maximum is four **first dispatches of distinct event IDs in any rolling 3600000 ms per recipient**, including all pages and delayed never-dispatched pages. The device persists the budget before it begins a first dispatch; crashes/unknown outcomes still spend it. Retry of an already dispatched ID does not spend a fresh token, but is independently bounded. Scope/stream changes, pause/resume, credential rotation or restart cannot reset the recipient's budget. A recipient cannot create alternate subscriptions to multiply it. Slower approved cadence applies in addition to the maximum; collection interval is not an Android scheduling promise.

Never-dispatched catch-up events are fresh. Select oldest eligible pages/groups first subject to the budget, age and scope; report eviction rather than manufacture a later observation time. Reconnect may coalesce device scheduling and network work, but cannot reclassify new events as retries. Catch-up selection is at most four events and 65536 UTF-8 JSON bytes including its wrapper; a group may span selections. The [batch schema](../../schemas/oap/0.1.0/batch.schema.json) describes a LOCAL selection result, not a mandatory cloud history/batch endpoint. The direct profile sends each selected event separately. Host-specific batching must preserve per-event validity, limits, budget and receipt semantics and may not imply fewer model wakeups than the host actually documents.

Every delivery cycle has at most six attempts including its first attempt. Wait after failed attempt n using full jitter uniformly from zero to min(300000, 2000 * 2^(n-1)) ms; attempt numbers 1..5 yield the next wait. A valid receiver retry hint acts as a minimum wait. The safe error body allows a hint up to 3600000 ms; a larger valid HTTP hint suspends affected attempts until that deadline, never causes an earlier retry by clamping or credential/capability repair. If the hint is beyond the remaining authorization/event lifetime, do not dispatch; expire normally. Network-unreachable periods do not spin or consume repeated attempts while no request starts. Use a bounded connection timeout of 10000 ms and complete request timeout of 30000 ms; conserve device wakeups under an offline signal.

Retry transport loss/timeouts/ambiguous receipts, HTTP 408/429 and 5xx subject to profile exceptions. Reject malformed/permanent scope/profile/version/identity/integrity errors without retrying the same invalid request. HTTP 401 or 403 marks credentials/authorization unusable and stops dispatch until an explicit supported local repair; do not automatically fall back to a different destination. HTTP 410 stops automatic dispatch and marks the endpoint unavailable unless its verified error specifically establishes event expiry; in that case expire/remove that event. HTTP 413 requires local visible profile/scope repair for future windows, not repartitioning/re-ID of the failed window. Invalid acknowledgments are ambiguous, not consumption evidence; six such failures exhaust a cycle.

After attempt six, retain eligible pending data as exhausted within its deadline. A new bounded cycle requires a recorded transition from offline to usable network, successful receiver capability/credential repair, or explicit user retry. New cycles are separated by at least 900000 ms per recipient; continuous network flapping cannot create an unbounded loop. Persist the cycle-trigger generation so a restart cannot replay it. There is no unconditional timer that starts a new cycle every time six attempts finish. At most one HTTP event dispatch is active per recipient; concurrency across recipients is separately bounded by local device capacity. Empty queues do not poll an activity endpoint.

## D4 Local cursors and gaps

An authenticated local read/select operation returns current-stream retained events and bounded gap ranges. At most four such selections may start in any rolling 60000 ms per recipient; persist this budget separately from fresh delivery/retry budgets. A cursor is an opaque unpredictable capability reference of at least 128 bits, bound to local caller identity, recipient, stream, scope revision, grant/subscription, selection position and expiry. Cursor use rechecks the current consent fence. A cursor never authorizes scope or extends retention; no grant means no read. Lifetime is at most the active subscription deadline and 24 hours, with a quota of 16 active cursors per recipient. Invalid/foreign cursors are cursor_invalid; deleted/expired positions return cursor_stale plus a safe active-scope gap indication after authorization. Old-scope cursors and old-scope gap detail are inaccessible following narrowing.

Sequence ranges record retained-history loss caused by `eviction`, `expired` or `explicit_delete`; they contain no app names, usage totals or private contents. Merge adjacent same-reason ranges. Keep at most 128 ranges per active stream within its existing history deadline and byte quota. On overflow collapse to a coarse safe missing range; never guess app usage or claim exact reason for merged mixed loss. If the gap schema cannot represent mixed reasons, omit detailed ranges, keep gap_count as the count of bounded retained gap records and expose history_unavailable plus a bounded local diagnostic indicating lost detail. Do not retain unbounded per-event tombstones. Group gaps preserve incomplete-window interpretation. Network loss alone is pending delivery, not observed-history loss.

Gaps are not a second high-volume event family in 0.1.0. They are local recovery/status data; a receiver also detects missing sequences/pages or expired incomplete groups. A host profile can surface authorized gap metadata only if its real contract supports it. Direct HTTPS has no invented remote status/history API. Deletion never starts collection or sends an activity notification as a side effect. Receiver copies already consumed are outside local deletion control.

## D5 Expiry, storage and clock changes

Retained aggregate/checkpoint/history/outbox state is capped at 4194304 bytes and a maximum original age of 86400000 ms per recipient, including wrappers/indexes/gaps and any plaintext-equivalent copies. User-selected tighter limits win. The same observation copied into queue/history does not receive another age allowance. Freeze deadlines from original window start, and enforce the earliest grant/subscription/credential deadline when finalizing or dispatching. Retry/renewal/projection never resets them. Raw permitted observations are transient; discard after aggregation/checkpoint, never later than the shortest applicable grant history or 24 hours. If overlapping grants exist, isolate per-recipient retained scope/deadlines; a broad grant cannot extend another recipient's copy.

Expiry is checked on access/dispatch, after startup, and during bounded cleanup. Evict oldest eligible windows/groups when over quota, leaving explicit active-scope gaps; do not evict arbitrary app entries and imply a complete summary. File/database free-space failure rejects new retention and reports storage_unavailable; it does not silently switch to in-memory infinite history or a hosted queue. Deduplication/consent markers and safe control records have separate bounded quotas; if safe accounting cannot fit, fail closed and require local cleanup.

Use elapsed/monotonic time for within-boot waits/budgets, persist UTC deadlines and the latest trusted clock/budget checkpoint for restart. Do not derive fresh age from time since replay. Detect wall-clock rollback/large discontinuity or uncertain reboot time. When correct expiry/cadence cannot be established, stop dispatch/affected collection, expose clock_untrusted and require a trustworthy local clock reconciliation; never restore expired grants or erase the persisted rolling-hour budget. Forward clock changes expire earlier. This is deliberately conservative and does not require an intermediary time service.

Safe local diagnostics are capped at seven days and 1048576 bytes per device, containing no activity or secret material. Retain local revoked-credential fingerprints/generation markers only until the affected credential's original expiry, then delete; at most 128 retained recipient/grant identities and 1024 credential markers per device. Reject additional pairing if safe quotas are exhausted. Removal cannot be undone by replaying an old credential, idempotency key or control record. Protected credential material is deleted immediately on disconnect; a hash/fingerprint is not a usable token.

## D6 Health and response stages

[status.schema.json](../../schemas/oap/0.1.0/status.schema.json) is local, recipient-scoped health: permission, consent state/revision, expiry, delivery state, queue/retention bounds, gap count and separately reported receipt/valid-consumption timestamps. It contains no application durations, goals, messages or credentials. User-facing sharing controls may show the explicit allowed-app scope from the protected grant; routine logs must not copy it. Suppressed zero windows can leave receipt timestamps unchanged; do not equate that with a network error.

Delivery states distinguish idle, pending, delivering, backoff, exhausted, blocked, offline, paused, revoked, expired, credential_unusable, permanent_error and unsupported. A bounded error record gives a machine code, safe message and retryability. Unknown/corrupt consent permits safe identity-scoped status only where its identity can be recovered, with no invented grant expiry; otherwise return a safe error. `last_receipt_at` means a verified transport acknowledgment; `last_valid_consumption_at` requires actual profile evidence. Agent evaluation and user notification belong to the chosen host and require separate evidence. No absent notification proves a delivery failure. Privacy-sensitive host errors are reduced to the safe local catalog. See [errors](errors.md).
